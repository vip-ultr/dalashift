1. Introduction &amp; Research Overview

The interview was introduced as part of university-led field research into strip
cropping, in which cereals and legumes are intercropped, and the development of a
tool to guide farming practice. The participant welcomed the team.

2. Farmer Profile &amp; Farming Experience

The participant described themselves as an experienced smallholder in the
validation area. Years of experience and family background are withheld.
Rainy-season crops reported included sorghum, maize, cowpeas, groundnuts and
tomatoes. Dry-season irrigation had been suspended because well water had
declined and was needed for community drinking and livestock.

3. Farm Size, Strip Cropping &amp; Fertilizer Management

Holding size and plot layout are withheld. The participant reported a
strip-cropping system in which cereals and legumes are grown in alternate rows
or strips, except for pure maize. Legumes were described as cash crops and
cereals as food-security crops.

4. Climate Change Impacts &amp; Adaptation Strategies

The account described early rains followed by dry spells. The participant's
stated response was to delay planting until the soil retained sufficient
moisture from later showers, so that crops would germinate and survive. Early
maturing and resilient varieties were also reported. Strip cropping was said to
suppress weeds, protect the soil surface and disrupt pest cycles because pests
cannot find large uniform blocks of a single host crop.

5. Technology Adoption &amp; Community Engagement

The participant said farmers are willing to adopt new practices when introduced
through proper community entry protocols, engaging village heads, ward heads and
agricultural extension officers, with lead farmers demonstrating results first.

6. GPS Consent

The participant consented to the team recording farm coordinates for research
purposes. No coordinates are published in this record.
