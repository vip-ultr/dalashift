Farmer Interview Transcription & Translation
Interviewee: Bello Yusuf Maitama  |  Location: Doka, Tofa, Kano State  |  Language: Translated from Hausa to English
1. Introduction & Research Overview
Interviewer: Peace be upon you, and the mercy and blessings of Allah. What brought us here is that these are students from Bayero University. They have come to meet with farmers under Professor Aminu Fagge.
Interviewer: They are here because of a tool they developed. They are going to ask you questions regarding strip cropping—where cereals and legumes are intercropped. Based on your answers, the tool will provide recommendations and guidance on how to adjust your farming practices to improve yields.
Respondent: Peace be upon you. Welcome, we are very pleased to have you.
2. Farmer Profile & Farming Experience
Interviewer: How many years have you been farming, what types of crops do you plant, and what is your background in farming?
Respondent: My name is Bello Yusuf Maitama from Doka, Tofa Local Government Area, Kano State. Regarding farming, I have been farming independently for about 30 to 35 years now, following in the footsteps of my family.
Interviewer: What types of crops do you plant on your farm?
Respondent: During the rainy season, we plant sorghum, maize, cowpeas, groundnuts, and tomatoes. We used to do dry-season farming (irrigation) as well, but because the water level in the wells dropped and the water is needed for community drinking and livestock, we suspended dry-season farming, especially for groundnuts, even though dry-season farming can be very profitable.
3. Farm Size, Strip Cropping & Fertilizer Management
Interviewer: What is the size estimate of your farmlands?
Respondent: My farmlands span roughly two to four hectares in total, though they are not located in one single contiguous piece. We practice strip cropping here, where we intercrop cereals and legumes in alternate rows or strips.
Interviewer: Do you practice crop rotation, or do you mix the crops together in the same field?
Respondent: Most of the time, except when I plant pure maize, I practice intercropping—planting sorghum alongside cowpeas or groundnuts. Legumes like groundnuts and cowpeas serve as our cash crops, while cereals like sorghum and maize are primarily used for family consumption and food security.
4. Climate Change Impacts & Adaptation Strategies
Interviewer: How has climate change and unpredictable rainfall affected your farming this year, and how did you respond?
Respondent: The rainy season came with surprises this year. The rains started early, but then we experienced prolonged dry spells (drought pockets). Those who rushed to plant without caution suffered losses when the rains stopped temporarily. However, I waited and delayed planting until the soil retained sufficient moisture from subsequent light showers, which allowed my crops to germinate successfully and survive the season.
Respondent: Because of climate shifts, we also rely on early-maturing and resilient seed varieties. Furthermore, strip cropping helps suppress weeds and protects the soil surface, while also disrupting pest cycles since pests cannot find large uniform blocks of a single host crop.
5. Technology Adoption & Community Engagement
Interviewer: We are developing a software tool that uses satellite data, soil moisture tracking, and rainfall forecasting to guide farmers on optimal planting times and crop selection. Do you think farmers in rural communities will accept and adopt this technology?
Respondent: Farmers are definitely willing to adopt new innovations, but it all depends on the approach used to introduce them. If you come directly with proper community entry protocols—engaging village heads (maigari), ward heads (mai unguwa), and local agricultural extension officers (malamin gona)—the community will trust and embrace it. If local lead farmers test it and succeed, others will naturally follow their example.
Interviewer: We would like to collect GPS coordinates of your farm to use as a test case for our software tool. Are you ready?
Respondent: We are completely ready. Whenever you are ready to take the GPS coordinates of the farm, we will lead you there directly.
