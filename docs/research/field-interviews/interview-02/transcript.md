1. Introduction &amp; Farm Profile

The participant described themselves as an experienced smallholder in the
validation area. Years of experience, holding size and plot counts are withheld.
Crops reported included maize, soybeans, groundnuts, peppers and tomatoes. Both
rainy and dry season farming were described, with wells used for irrigation
because no dam is available.

2. Intercropping, Strip Cropping &amp; Fertilizer Management

A structured strip-cropping system was described, with cereals and legumes in
strips separated by a millet boundary line known locally as *kalankuwa*. Exact
strip compositions and ridge counts are withheld as identifying detail. Soybeans
were reported not to require chemical fertiliser, while maize and groundnuts
were reported to need it. Application counts are withheld.

3. Crop Rotation &amp; Yield Improvements from Training

The participant was unambiguous that crop rotation is practised and attributed
the practice to agricultural training received through the university
partnership. A crop-level rotation rule was supplied in the participant's own
words: change the crop each season, rotate among cereals and millets, and bring
an earlier crop back later. A reported improvement in harvest was attributed to
the training. Exact yields and time periods are withheld as identifying detail.

4. Adapting to Rainfall Shifts &amp; Short-Duration Seeds

The season described was short, with a long dry spell. The response, agreed
collectively among farmers, was to switch to early-maturing, short-duration
varieties because long-duration crops did not fully mature. The stated planting
rule was to plant immediately on the first rains rather than wait. A
shorter-duration legume was offered as a lower-water alternative to a
higher-demand crop actually planted. Specific variety durations are withheld.

5. Community Adoption &amp; Satellite Forecasting Application

The idea of a 30-day rainfall outlook was received with strong interest. The
advice farmers already act on comes from radio weather forecasts: plant
early-maturing varieties, or switch from sorghum to cowpeas, based on expected
rainfall. The participant said beneficial innovations are shared once results
are seen, and that visitors from neighbouring areas ask about crops and improved
seeds. They direct such visitors to the agricultural extension officer.

6. GPS Consent

The participant consented to the team visiting to record farm coordinates for
research purposes. No coordinates are published in this record.
