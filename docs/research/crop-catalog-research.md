# Crop Catalog Agronomy Evidence

**Scope:** rain-fed catalog candidates reported in the Doka, Tofa interviews.
**Sources checked:** 2026-11-14.
**Status:** research evidence only. These are not validated recommendation thresholds.

## Interpretation

Samaru is in the Northern Guinea Savanna (NGS), while Tofa is in Kano's Sudan Savanna. Niger's Sahel is more distant still. These studies establish useful hypotheses and contextual comparisons, not automatic crop requirements for Tofa. Farmer interview statements are evidence of reported local practice, not general agronomic fact. Do not load any claim as a hard constraint without qualified agronomist review.

## Evidence that may inform catalog research

### Seasonal water need, general reference only

FAO's Irrigation Water Management manual gives indicative total crop-water-need ranges: maize 500–800 mm, groundnut 500–700 mm, sorghum/millet 450–650 mm, soybean 450–700 mm, pepper 600–900 mm, and tomato 400–800 mm. FAO describes these as rough estimates for cases where a more accurate calculation is unavailable and says the range depends on crop duration. These are crop evapotranspiration needs, not rainfall requirements, observed Tofa rainfall, or hard suitability boundaries. Do not compare them directly to seasonal precipitation without accounting for effective rainfall, soil water storage, runoff, planting date and duration. Sources: Brouwer & Heibloem, Irrigation Water Management: Training Manual No. 3, chapter 3, FAO, 1986, [chapter 3](https://www.fao.org/4/s2022e/s2022e07.htm), [chapter 2](https://www.fao.org/4/U3160E/u3160e04.htm).

The same manual gives crop coefficients by growth stage and explains crop evapotranspiration as ETcrop = Kc × ETo. Kc depends on climate, humidity and wind. These are reference-method inputs, not fixed daily water demand, and do not establish irrigation requirement or rain-fed suitability in Tofa.

### Northern Nigerian seasonal context

The Kano-Katsina-Maradi Sudan Savanna field-characterisation report describes farmer practice in sampled communities: sorghum and millet commonly planted late May to mid-June, cowpea late June to late July, and other sampled crops from late May to mid-July. It documents continuous cultivation, cereal-legume relay intercropping and legume rotations as distinct practices. This is regional survey evidence, not a Tofa planting recommendation. Full bibliographic metadata and source-host validation remain necessary. [Available report copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/264206241_Biophysical_Characterisation_of_Crop_Fields_in_Sudan_Savannah_Zone_of_North_Western_Nigeria_Kano-Katsina-Maradi_Pilot_Learning_Site_Sudan_Savanna_Agro-Ecological_Zone_Innovation_Platform).

A Kano study examined millet yield against rainfall indices at state scale, and an ABU thesis analysed rainfall variability and millet/sorghum yields across the Sudan Savanna, including a derived historical mean onset at Kano. These are climatology/yield associations, not crop physiological requirements or farm-specific planting dates. Do not turn the thesis dates into current triggers without checking its methods, period and local validation. [Kano millet rainfall-index paper](https://jees.fudutsinma.edu.ng/index.php/jees/article/view/9); [ABU thesis record](https://kubanni.abu.edu.ng/items/56b8c77c-a618-4649-95de-66fc2d3054f0).

### Northern Guinea Savanna field trials

Yusuf et al. (2009) tested maize after cowpea and soybean against maize and fallow in Samaru, NGS, during 2003–2005. The genotypes were cowpea IT 96D-724 and SAMPEA-7, soybean TGx 1448-2E and SAMSOY-2, and maize Oba Super 2. Maize following legumes yielded on average 1.2 and 1.3 times maize after fallow and maize after maize, respectively, in those experiments. The paper distinguishes fixed-N effects from other rotation effects and reports wide variation by legume N balance. This supports a rotation hypothesis, but Samaru's reported mean annual rainfall (1,011 ± 161 mm) and NGS setting differ from Tofa. Do not encode guaranteed yield benefit, nitrogen credit or fertilizer reduction. [Yusuf et al. (2009), DOI 10.1007/s10705-008-9232-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10705-008-9232-9); [IITA full text](https://biblio.iita.org/documents/S09ArtYusufGrainInthomDev.pdf-4a6d98cb7b0ab82c486a00402ba394f9.pdf).

An IAR Samaru thesis reports a long-term experiment comparing maize-maize, maize-cowpea and maize-soybean rotations, tillage and nitrogen rates. Site context is Samaru NGS, Alfisol/Typic Haplustalf, mean annual rainfall 1,011 ± 161 mm, with the described observation in wet season 2013. This confirms the sequences were experimentally studied; findings and complete citation metadata need extraction before claims are used. [IAR/ABU thesis](https://kubanni-backend.abu.edu.ng/server/api/core/bitstreams/d9815ca7-448d-455d-a90b-134a171242be/content).

### Crop-specific fields not verified for Tofa

No defensible Tofa-specific values for temperature limits, soil pH, texture, rooting depth, maturity by variety, nutrient requirement, fixation amount, residue load or disease-break status were established in this pass. Leave these unset. The 80–90 and 100–120 day sorghum maturity observations in interview 03 are one farmer's variety-specific experience, not catalog ranges.
